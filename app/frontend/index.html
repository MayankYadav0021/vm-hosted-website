ABOUT THE PROJECT

Research Overview

Large Language Models differ significantly in their computational requirements and inference behaviour. Deploying these models in resource-constrained environments requires an understanding of throughput, latency, memory consumption and optimization techniques.

This project develops an experimental benchmarking environment to evaluate multiple language models, investigate inference optimization strategies and demonstrate parameter-efficient fine-tuning. Experiments are conducted on CPU-based AWS EC2 infrastructure, with results recorded for comparison.

RESEARCH MOTIVATION

Problem Statement

Running language models efficiently on limited computational resources presents challenges involving model size, inference speed and memory requirements. This research investigates how model selection, numerical precision, batch processing and parameter-efficient adaptation affect these constraints.

RESEARCH SUMMARY

Conclusion & Future Scope

Key observations, experimental limitations and opportunities for further research.

Research Conclusion

This project establishes an experimental environment for comparing language-model inference performance, evaluating optimization strategies and demonstrating parameter-efficient fine-tuning on CPU infrastructure. The recorded experiments provide measurements of throughput, inference time and memory consumption under the tested configurations.

Research Limitations

  • Experiments were conducted on a CPU-based environment without GPU acceleration.
  • Results reflect the tested models, workloads and hardware configuration.
  • A formal baseline-versus-LoRA output-quality comparison has not been recorded.
  • Performance may vary on different hardware and software configurations.

Future Scope

  • Compare CPU and GPU inference performance.
  • Evaluate additional model architectures and quantization methods.
  • Expand workload sizes and benchmark repetitions.
  • Introduce formal language-quality evaluation metrics.
  • Extend infrastructure monitoring and experiment history.
